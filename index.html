Step to Light

Illuminating the path ahead

Kinetic tiles turn footsteps into electricity for footpath lights near Fairfield station. Try it below.

1. Walk the path

Tap a tile to step on it. At night, the lights ahead of you brighten.

Each step makes about 1-2.5 joules. That runs one dimmed light for only a second or two, so a solar panel does most of the charging.

2. Inside one tile

Press the tile and watch what happens inside.

  1. Step: the top plate moves down about 5 mm.
  2. Generate: magnets (yellow) drop into copper coils and make electricity.
  3. Count: the disc in the middle (teal) counts the step.
  4. Store: the electricity charges a 12 V battery.
  5. Reset: springs push the plate back up.

Our assumptions

  • Commercial tiles make 2-5 J per step. We assume ours makes half: 1-2.5 J.
  • About 4,000 people cross each day, 5 tile steps each.
  • 8 lights, dimmed to 1 W all night. The 2 lights nearest a walker brighten to 5 W for about 10 seconds.
  • The solar panel gets 3 (winter) to 6 (summer) hours of full sun, with 30% losses.
  • The batteries hold about 143 Wh.
